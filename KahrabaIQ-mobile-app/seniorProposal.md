1. Problem Statement
Residential buildings consume a significant amount of electrical energy due to
inefficient usage patterns, lack of intelligent control, and heavy reliance on
manual operation. In many homes, electrical appliances such as air conditioners,
lighting, and power sockets remain active even when spaces are unoccupied,
leading to unnecessary energy waste and increased electricity costs. Existing
smart home solutions mainly focus on monitoring and remote control, rather
than actively reducing energy consumption through intelligent decision-making.
In addition, most residential electrical systems are reactive rather than proactive.
Electrical overloads and fire-related hazards are often detected only after
dangerous conditions occur, increasing safety risks. There is limited integration
between energy management, occupancy detection, and emergency electrical
control in current systems. This project aims to address these issues by
developing an AI-driven smart energy control system that actively manages and
optimizes electrical usage. The scope of the project is limited to a single-room
prototype, where energy consumption, occupancy behavior, and safety
conditions are analyzed to enable intelligent control actions. By learning user
behavior and predicting inefficient or unsafe conditions, the system seeks to
reduce energy consumption, improve electrical safety, and demonstrate a
scalable solution suitable for future residential deployment.

3. Project Significance
This project is significant as it addresses two critical challenges in modern
residential environments: excessive energy consumption and electrical safety
risks. By focusing on intelligent control rather than passive monitoring, the
proposed system contributes to reducing unnecessary electricity usage, which
directly benefits individuals through lower energy costs and improved awareness
of their consumption behavior. From an organizational and societal perspective,
energy efficiency is a key component of sustainable development and
environmental protection. Reducing residential energy waste helps decrease
overall demand on power grids and lowers carbon emissions. Additionally,
integrating predictive safety mechanisms such as overload prevention and
emergency electrical shutdown enhances residential safety and reduces the risk
of fire-related incidents. Academically, the project is important as it
demonstrates the practical application of artificial intelligence, IoT, and
embedded systems in solving real-world engineering problems. The developed
prototype provides a scalable foundation that can be extended to larger
residential or commercial environments, making it relevant to future smart city
and energy management initiatives.
4. Brief Literature Review
Recent studies have shown that smart energy management systems can
significantly reduce residential energy consumption when combined with
occupancy detection and automated control mechanisms. Research on IoT-based
smart homes highlights the effectiveness of sensor-driven systems in monitoring
energy usage, but emphasizes that traditional rule-based automation is often
insufficient for optimal energy efficiency. More recent literature explores the use
of artificial intelligence and machine learning techniques to analyze user
behavior and predict energy demand. These approaches enable systems to adapt
over time, improving decision accuracy and reducing unnecessary power usage.
Studies also indicate that occupancy-aware control of high-energy appliances,
such as air conditioning systems, can lead to substantial energy savings. In
addition, research on intelligent electrical safety systems demonstrates that
integrating fire detection sensors with automatic electrical shutdown
mechanisms can significantly reduce fire risks. However, many existing solutions
treat energy management and safety as separate systems. This project builds
upon existing research by integrating AI-based energy optimization and
electrical safety control into a unified, intelligent platform.
